Semantic search embeddings

The lesson: keyword search breaks on vocabulary mismatch — when the user's words differ from the document's. Semantic search reads through the words to the meaning. Production systems usually run both and fuse the rankings (hybrid search), because keyword is unbeatable for exact terms like error codes, SKUs and names.

First-stage retrieval is fast but blunt — it grabs a shortlist. A reranker then reads the question and each candidate together. Watch the right card move when the system stops asking “same topic?” and starts asking “does this answer the question?”

The mechanism, in one sentence: a bi-encoder embeds the question and the chunk separately and compares two vectors — that is stage 1, and by the time it compares them the actual words are long gone. A cross-encoder reads the question and the chunk together, in one pass, so it can ask whether this text answers that question. Far more accurate, far too slow to run over a whole corpus, ideal over a shortlist of twenty. In real systems this is often the single biggest quality jump for the least work.

Chunking has a cost nobody mentions: a chunk is torn out of its document and loses what it was about. "This does not cover…" — this what? Contextual Retrieval (Anthropic's technique) fixes it by having an LLM write a one-line situating blurb for each chunk, prepending it, and embedding that. Toggle it and watch the ranking flip.

This is what separates a demo from a system. The rule that matters: measure retrieval and generation separately. Here's why — drag k down to 1 and watch faithfulness stay green while the answer quietly goes half-wrong.

question "How long do the Aeronote and its stylus last on a charge?"
k = 2
Metrics
What retrieval handed the model
The trap, stated plainly: at k = 1 both generation metrics are green — faithfulness 0.91, answer relevancy 0.90. Every claim really is supported, and the answer really is on topic. The generator did nothing wrong. But retrieval never handed it the stylus chunk, so the answer silently omits half the question. A generation-only dashboard is entirely green through this failure. Only context recall catches it — and context recall is the metric teams skip, because unlike the other three it requires someone to sit down and label which chunks should have been retrieved for each question. That labelling is the actual work of RAG evaluation.
And the other end: push k to 5 and recall is perfect, but context precision collapses to 0.40 and faithfulness slips too. More context is not free — you pay in tokens, in latency, and in the "lost in the middle" effect, where models under-use material buried in the middle of a long prompt. k is a tuned parameter, not a default. This is what a retrieval eval is for.
