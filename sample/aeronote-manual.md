# Aeronote — Product Guide & Policies

*Aeronote Ltd · Rev. 4 · internal*

## 1. About Aeronote

### 1.1 What Aeronote is

Aeronote is a paper-like tablet built for people who think with a pen. It combines a matte,
glare-free reading surface with a pressure-sensitive stylus, so notes taken on it look and feel like
ink on paper while staying searchable, backed up, and exportable like any other file. It is not a
general-purpose tablet: there is no web browser, no app store, and no video playback. Everything
about the hardware and the software is built around two tasks — writing and reading — and doing
both of them without the glow, glare, and distraction of a conventional screen.

### 1.2 Who it's for

Aeronote suits three kinds of owner equally well: a student who wants one place for lecture notes,
readings, and past-paper practice; a professional who takes meeting notes and wants them searchable
months later; and a reader who prefers a single dedicated device for long-form PDFs and ebooks
instead of a phone screen. It is not aimed at anyone who wants to sketch in colour, edit video, or
run third-party apps — the device deliberately does none of that.

## 2. The device

### 2.1 Endurance and power

The device's internal power cell lasts roughly three weeks of typical daily note-taking before it
needs topping up again — figure on daily use of an hour or two of writing and reading combined.
Topping it up from empty takes about ninety minutes over USB-C, and a ten-minute top-up is usually
enough to get through a full day if you're caught short. Standby drain is minimal: an Aeronote left
untouched in a bag for a week loses only a few percent.

### 2.2 Size and weight

The Aeronote weighs 375 grams and is about as thick as ten sheets of paper stacked together, so it
slips into a bag or under an arm without the bulk of a laptop. Held in one hand for a long reading
session it stays noticeably lighter than a comparable tablet, which is a deliberate design choice —
the team benchmarked it against a stack of paperback novels rather than against other tablets.

### 2.3 The reading surface

The Aeronote display is a 10.3-inch e-ink panel with a textured matte finish that mimics the drag of
a pen on paper. Because e-ink reflects ambient light instead of emitting its own, the surface stays
readable outdoors in direct sunlight and produces none of the glare or eye strain associated with
backlit screens. The tradeoff is refresh speed: the display is built for text and handwriting, not
for video or fast scrolling animation.

### 2.4 Buttons and ports

There is a single physical button on the top edge, used to wake the device and, held for ten
seconds, to force a restart. The only port is USB-C, used for charging and for a wired transfer of
files to a computer when Wi-Fi isn't available. There is no headphone jack and no SD card slot — all
storage is internal and all backup happens over the network.

### 2.5 The stylus

The stylus that ships with every Aeronote has no batteries of its own: it draws power wirelessly
from a small charging cradle built into the right edge of the tablet, and a full charge lasts
roughly a full day of continuous writing. It supports four thousand levels of pressure sensitivity
and a tilt sensor for shading, and the nib is a replaceable felt tip designed to wear at roughly the
rate of a pencil, not a hard plastic stylus tip.

## 3. Setting it up

### 3.1 What's in the box

Every Aeronote ships with the tablet itself, the stylus, a USB-C cable, and a quick-start card. The
base model does not include a case; a folio cover is available separately or bundled with the
premium package described in the pricing chapter. There is no printed manual in the box — the device
links to the online guide the first time it connects to Wi-Fi.

### 3.2 First power-on

Press and hold the top button for three seconds to wake the device for the first time. A short setup
walks through language, time zone, and handwriting sample calibration — writing a single sentence so
the recognition engine can adapt to your handwriting from day one. The whole process takes about five
minutes.

### 3.3 Connecting to Wi-Fi

Aeronote needs a network connection to create an account, sync notes, and receive firmware updates,
though it can be used entirely offline once set up — notes simply queue locally and sync the next
time it sees a network. Setup supports standard home and office Wi-Fi; enterprise networks that
require a browser-based login page are not supported at first connection, though they can usually be
added afterward from the settings menu.

### 3.4 Creating an account

An Aeronote account is what makes sync, backup, and export possible. Signing up needs only an email
address and a password — no phone number, no separate app download. A single account can be signed
into more than one Aeronote, which is common in households with more than one device, and notebooks
can optionally be shared between accounts on the same plan.

## 4. Taking notes

### 4.1 Starting a notebook

New notebooks are created from the home screen with a single tap and can be organised into folders,
renamed at any time, and reordered by dragging. There is no limit on the number of notebooks beyond
available storage, and an empty notebook takes up a negligible amount of space until pages are
written on.

### 4.2 Page types and templates

Every notebook page can be set to one of several templates — blank, ruled, grid, dot-grid, or a
Cornell-style layout split for notes and a summary column. Templates can be mixed within a single
notebook, so a lecture notebook might use ruled pages for notes and a dot-grid page for the diagrams
that come up in a particular week.

### 4.3 Handwriting recognition

Handwriting on any page can be converted to typed text on demand, without changing what's stored —
the original strokes are always kept, and the typed conversion is a view on top of them, not a
replacement. Recognition supports over thirty languages and improves the more it sees an individual's
handwriting, which is what the calibration step during setup is for.

### 4.4 Searching your notes

Because handwriting is recognised in the background as it's written, the search bar on the home
screen searches across every notebook by content, not just by title — a name jotted in the margin of
a meeting three months ago is findable the same way a typed document would be. Search also covers
text recognised inside imported PDFs, not only handwritten pages.

## 5. Reading and export

### 5.1 Reading PDFs and books

Aeronote reads PDFs, EPUB books, and its own native notebook format side by side in the same library
view. A PDF opened for reading can be annotated directly with the stylus — underlining, margin notes,
and highlights are all stored as a separate ink layer on top of the original file, so the source PDF
itself is never modified.

### 5.2 Notes and export

Every notebook syncs automatically to the cloud whenever a network connection is available, and any
page — or an entire notebook — can be exported at any time as a PDF or as plain recognised text.
Exported files can be sent by email directly from the device or saved to a connected cloud storage
folder, and nothing about export requires a computer.

### 5.3 Sharing a single page

An individual page can be shared as an image or a PDF without exporting the whole notebook around
it, which is the usual way a single diagram or a single day's notes gets sent to someone else. Shared
pages carry no watermark and no reference back to the sending account.

## 6. Software and updates

### 6.1 Firmware updates

Firmware updates are delivered automatically over Wi-Fi roughly once a month and install overnight
while the device is charging, so they rarely interrupt a working session. Each update is described
in a short changelog visible in settings before it installs, and a device can be held back a version
manually if an update needs to be delayed.

### 6.2 Release notes

Firmware version 1.2 added the calligraphy pen style and a battery usage graph in settings, and was
the first release to support holding a device back a version manually.

### 6.3 Release notes

Firmware version 1.3 added the soft pencil texture and two new reading fonts optimised for the
e-ink panel, alongside a handful of small stability fixes to overnight sync.

### 6.4 Release notes

Firmware version 1.4 added the Cornell-style page template described in the page-templates section
and a background-recognition speed improvement that roughly halved the delay before newly written
text becomes searchable.

### 6.5 Release notes

Firmware version 1.5 added folder colour tags, a quick-search shortcut reachable from the lock
screen, and support for renaming a notebook without opening it first.

### 6.6 Release notes

Firmware version 1.6 added offline dictionary lookups for handwriting recognition and a
left-handed writing mode that mirrors interface elements to the opposite edge.

### 6.7 Release notes

Firmware version 1.7 added export to Markdown alongside the existing PDF and text options, and a
redesigned settings menu grouped by task rather than by feature name.

### 6.8 The companion app

A free companion app for desktop and phone mirrors the notebook library and keeps it in sync, and is
the easiest way to browse and export a large number of notes without paging through them on the
device itself. The companion app is optional — Aeronote is fully usable without ever installing it —
but most owners with more than a handful of notebooks find it convenient for search and backup.

## 7. Buying Aeronote

### 7.1 Pricing and bundles

The Aeronote base model costs $399 and ships with the tablet, the stylus, and a USB-C cable. A
premium bundle adds a folio cover and two extra pen tips for $449 total. Both configurations include
the same tablet hardware and the same access to firmware updates and cloud sync — the premium bundle
changes only what's in the box, not the device's capability.

### 7.2 Where to buy

Aeronote sells directly through its own online store and through a small number of authorised
electronics retailers listed on the website. Devices bought secondhand can still create an account
and receive firmware updates, though any promotional accessories from the original purchase are not
transferable.

### 7.3 Trade-in

Owners of an older Aeronote can trade it in toward a new one; the trade-in value depends on the
device's condition and is quoted online before anything is shipped. A trade-in device must power on
and pass a basic screen check to qualify for the quoted value.

## 8. Shipping

### 8.1 Delivery times

Orders placed on the direct store typically ship within two business days and arrive within a week
domestically. International orders are handled by regional distribution partners and delivery times
vary by country, generally between one and three weeks.

### 8.2 Tracking and delays

A tracking link is emailed as soon as an order ships. Delays beyond the estimated window are
uncommon but do happen around major holidays, when carrier volume is highest; the support team can
usually give an updated estimate if a tracking link hasn't moved in several days.

## 9. Returns and refunds

### 9.1 The return window

You can return your Aeronote for any reason within forty-five days of delivery and get a full
refund, no questions asked, no explanation needed. This does not apply to a unit with a cracked
screen, water damage, or a missing stylus — those go through the repair process described in the
troubleshooting chapter instead.

### 9.2 How refunds are processed

Once a returned device is received and inspected, a refund is issued to the original payment method
within five to seven business days. Return shipping is free within the country of purchase using the
prepaid label generated automatically when a return is started from the account page.

### 9.3 Condition for a return

A returned device should include the stylus and cable it shipped with, but does not need its original
packaging. Light cosmetic wear from normal use does not affect eligibility for a full refund; the
45-day window and the cracked-screen exception above are the only two conditions that matter.

## 10. Troubleshooting

### 10.1 The device won't turn on

If the screen shows nothing at all, the most common cause is simply an empty power cell — connect
the USB-C cable and wait ten minutes before trying the top button again, since a fully drained unit
needs a short charge before it can display anything, including a charging icon.

### 10.2 The screen has stopped responding

If the screen stops responding to touch or the stylus while still displaying a page, error code E-42
means the digitizer — the layer that senses the pen — needs a reset. Hold the top button for ten
seconds until the screen goes blank, then release and press it once more to restart; this clears the
condition in the large majority of cases without any data loss.

### 10.3 Wi-Fi won't connect

If the device can't join a network it previously connected to, forgetting the network in settings and
re-adding the password usually resolves it. Enterprise networks with a browser login page are the
most common cause of a repeated failure, since first-time setup doesn't support them; adding the
network from the settings menu after setup instead of during it usually works.

### 10.4 The stylus isn't detected

If writing produces no ink, first check that the stylus is seated in its charging cradle for at
least thirty seconds — a fully drained stylus won't pair even when it's placed correctly at first.
If it still isn't detected afterward, restarting the tablet from the settings menu re-establishes the
pairing in almost every remaining case.

### 10.5 A firmware update failed partway

If a firmware update fails to complete, the device automatically reverts to the previous working
version rather than being left in a partial state, so a failed update never bricks the device.
Leaving it on its charging cradle overnight lets the update retry automatically the next time it's
scheduled to run.

### 10.6 The power cell is draining faster than expected

Faster-than-normal drain is almost always background sync running more often than usual, which
happens for a day or two after a large number of pages are added at once, such as importing a big
PDF library. It settles back to the usual pace within a couple of days on its own.

### 10.7 Contacting support

If none of the steps above resolve an issue, support can be reached from the help section of the
companion app or by email, and a typical response arrives within one business day. Having the
device's serial number ready, found in settings under "About," speeds up any support conversation.

## 11. Care and maintenance

### 11.1 Cleaning the screen

The matte surface can be wiped with a dry, soft cloth; a microfibre cloth used for glasses works
well. Liquid cleaners of any kind should be avoided, since they can seep under the edge of the
display and cause permanent staining rather than being safely wiped away.

### 11.2 Storage

For storage longer than a few weeks, leaving the device at roughly half power rather than fully
charged or fully drained keeps the internal cell in better long-term condition, which is standard
practice for the kind of cell used in most portable electronics.

### 11.3 Protecting against drops and water

Aeronote is not rated for submersion and a drop directly onto a corner is the most common cause of a
cracked screen. A folio cover meaningfully reduces the risk from an ordinary drop, and the pricing
chapter above describes how to add one to an order.

## 12. Accessories

### 12.1 Folio cover

The folio cover attaches magnetically, doubles as a stand at two viewing angles, and adds minimal
thickness to the device. It's included in the premium bundle and available separately for anyone who
bought the base model.

### 12.2 Extra pen tips

Replacement felt nibs come in packs of three and are a simple twist-off, twist-on swap that takes
under a minute. A well-used nib is worth replacing once writing starts to feel noticeably scratchier
than when the stylus was new.

### 12.3 Screen protectors

A textured screen protector is available for anyone who wants extra durability without changing the
writing feel; the stock surface is already matte, so the protector is about scratch resistance rather
than glare reduction, which the panel already handles on its own.

## 13. Specifications

### 13.1 Display and body

The display is a 10.3-inch e-ink panel at 300 pixels per inch. The body measures 195 by 145 by 4.6
millimetres and weighs 375 grams. Available body colours are slate grey and warm white.

### 13.2 Storage and connectivity

Aeronote ships with 32 gigabytes of internal storage, enough for tens of thousands of handwritten
pages alongside a substantial PDF and ebook library. Connectivity is Wi-Fi only — there is no
cellular option — plus USB-C for charging and wired transfer.

## 14. Compliance and certification

### 14.1 Regulatory certification

Aeronote is certified for sale in the regions it ships to, carrying the standard electromagnetic
compatibility and radio-equipment certifications required in each market. Certification marks are
printed on the underside of the device and listed in full on the support site.

### 14.2 Recycling and disposal

The device should be disposed of through a local electronics recycling program rather than general
waste, in line with regional e-waste regulations. The internal power cell is not designed to be
removed by the owner; a recycling facility handles cell removal safely as part of standard
processing.
